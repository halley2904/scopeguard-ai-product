# ScopeGuard - User Research

**Status:** Research not yet completed  
**Method:** Asynchronous interviews / written responses

## Research objective

Validate or falsify the current hypothesis that freelancers struggle to determine exactly how a new client request differs from the original agreed scope, and understand how they currently handle that situation.

## Current hypothesis

Freelancers may recognize that a request has expanded beyond the original agreement but lack a compact, evidence-backed way to identify exactly what changed and communicate the boundary confidently.

This is a hypothesis, not a validated finding.

## Research questions

1. How do freelancers currently record and reference agreed scope?
2. How often do clients request work that was not explicitly included?
3. How do freelancers determine whether a request is actually outside scope?
4. What happens when the freelancer believes a request is outside scope?
5. What makes it difficult to raise the issue with the client?
6. Would evidence from the original agreement make that conversation easier?
7. What would make an AI-generated assessment trustworthy enough to use?
8. What types of mistakes would make the product unusable?

## Async interview script

> Hey! I'm researching how freelancers handle client requests that may go beyond the original project scope. No call needed — I'd really appreciate a few answers here. I'm not selling anything; I'm trying to understand the workflow.
>
> 1. How often do clients ask for work that wasn't in the original scope?
> 2. What usually happens when that occurs?
> 3. How do you currently check what was included in the original agreement?
> 4. What's the most frustrating part of handling a scope change?
> 5. Has a scope change ever cost you time, money, or created conflict with a client?

## Research rules

- Do not lead participants toward the ScopeGuard hypothesis.
- Ask about current behavior before showing the prototype.
- Record contradictory evidence as carefully as supporting evidence.
- Do not convert a single participant's experience into a general product requirement.
- Separate observed behavior from interpretation.

## What would change the product hypothesis?

The hypothesis should be reconsidered if research shows that:

- freelancers rarely encounter scope ambiguity;
- existing tools already solve the problem adequately;
- the primary pain is pricing/negotiation rather than identifying the change;
- users do not trust AI to assess agreements;
- or another adjacent problem is consistently more important.
